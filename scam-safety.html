Shut down scam calls

Any number can be faked. These are the patterns that give scammers away — and the fastest way to report them.

One-ring

Wangiri (“one ring and cut”)

A missed call from an unfamiliar foreign number. Calling back connects to a premium-rate line that bills you per minute. Don’t call back numbers you don’t recognise.

Spoofing

Fake caller ID

Scammers display a real bank, police or local number. Hang up and call the organisation back on the number printed on your card or its official site.

OTP theft

“Read me the code”

No genuine bank, carrier or delivery firm asks you to read out a one-time passcode. Anyone who does is taking over your account.

Urgency

Pay now or else

Threats of arrest, account closure or disconnection, demanding gift cards, crypto or wire transfer, are textbook fraud.

Delivery

Parcel & customs fees

Texts or calls about a held parcel with a small “fee” link harvest card details. Track parcels only on the courier’s own app.

Remote access

“Tech support” calls

Unsolicited callers asking you to install screen-sharing software want control of your device and bank sessions.

Already answered or paid? Do this now

  1. Call your bank on its official number and freeze cards / transfers.
  2. Change passwords and turn on two-factor authentication for email and banking.
  3. If you installed remote-access software, disconnect from the internet and uninstall it.
  4. Ask your mobile operator about call-barring for premium and international numbers.
  5. Report it to the official channel below — reports help block ranges.
