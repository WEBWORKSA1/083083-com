Number decoder

Country and prefix from the public numbering plan, plus patterns, a memorability score and cultural lore. Everything runs in your browser — nothing is sent or stored.

How the score works

Memorability, 1–100

Fewer distinct digits, long repeat runs, mirrored halves, palindromes and ascending steps all make a number easier to recall. Lucky digits (8, 6, 9) add a little; 4 subtracts, reflecting East Asian buyer preferences.

Privacy

We never look anyone up

The decoder reads only the structure of the digits. It doesn’t query any database of people, and no number you type leaves your device.

Accuracy

Allocation ≠ current network

Prefix data shows who a range was assigned to. Portability means a number can move. Always confirm with your carrier for anything that matters.

What makes a phone number valuable?

Businesses pay real money for memorable numbers because recall drives calls. A number seen once on a van or billboard has to survive a few seconds in short-term memory. Repeats (888), mirrors (083 083), and sequences (1234) compress into fewer “chunks”, which is why they sell as golden numbers in most markets.

Signals the decoder checks

  • Distinct digits — fewer is easier.
  • Longest repeat run — 3+ identical digits stands out.
  • Mirrored halves & palindromes — symmetry aids recall.
  • Lucky/unlucky digits — matters to buyers in China, Hong Kong, Singapore and diaspora markets.
