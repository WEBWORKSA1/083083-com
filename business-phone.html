Features that matter (and ones that don’t)

  • Must-have: auto-attendant/IVR, ring groups, voicemail-to-email, mobile + desktop apps, number porting.
  • High-value: call recording, CRM integration, business SMS, analytics, AI call summaries.
  • Often oversold: desk-phone bundles for remote teams, video seats nobody uses, long contract discounts that lock you in.

What drives cost

Pricing is usually per user per month, with discounts for annual billing. The real total depends on: number of seats, which tier unlocks recording/CRM, international minutes, extra numbers (local, toll-free, vanity), contact-centre add-ons, and hardware. Always ask for a written total cost for 12 and 36 months.

Vanity & golden numbers

A memorable number (repeats like 083 083, 888 888, or words on a keypad) lifts recall on vehicles, radio and outdoor ads. Most providers sell premium number inventory; availability varies by country and range. Score a number’s memorability →

Buyer’s checklist

  1. List must-have features and number of users today and in 12 months.
  2. Test call quality on a free trial from your real internet connection.
  3. Confirm porting timeline for your existing numbers.
  4. Get 12- and 36-month written totals.
  5. Check contract exit terms and price-rise clauses.
  6. Confirm emergency-calling support for your location.
